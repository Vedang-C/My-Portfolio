Food Delivery Platform Design Process

Problem Statement

The goal was to design a mobile-first food delivery platform tailored to fast-paced, urban lifestyles. Users often faced frustration with long wait times, clunky app flows, and lack of clarity around order tracking. The challenge was to streamline the user experience, improve discoverability of menu items, simplify checkout, and increase transparency around delivery timing.

Design Thinking

We followed a user-centered design thinking framework: Empathize (understand the user context), Define (identify the real problem), Ideate (brainstorm creative solutions), Prototype (build testable versions), and Test (gather feedback and iterate). This cycle ensured that user insights continuously shaped the solution.

User Research

We interviewed 8 users across three demographics: students, working professionals, and busy parents. The interviews revealed major pain points: long and complex checkout forms, unclear pricing breakdowns, difficulty finding specific dishes, and minimal feedback during order tracking. Users preferred intuitive filters, personalized suggestions, and real-time delivery updates. We also conducted competitor analysis of apps like Uber Eats and DoorDash to identify common UX patterns and missed opportunities.

Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Initial sketches were drawn by hand to explore the layout of core screens: Home (with categories and promotions), Menu Listing, Product Details, Cart, and Order Tracking. Our goal was to minimize cognitive load—only the most essential elements were placed on each screen. We used grayscale boxes to block out UI sections, focusing on structure before styling. Key decisions included prominent CTA buttons, tab navigation, and collapsing sections for optional info.

High-Fidelity Prototype

Based on feedback from the sketches, we created high-fidelity screens in Figma. The interface used brand colors and micro-interactions to guide users naturally through the flow. Icons and typography were chosen for clarity on smaller devices. We added animation transitions between steps like selecting items, modifying cart contents, and tracking the delivery live. We ensured accessibility standards were met, including sufficient contrast, tap-friendly UI elements, and screen reader labels.

Usability Testing

We tested our interactive prototype with 5 participants using remote screen-sharing tools. We assigned them tasks such as "Order a vegan lunch combo" or "Track your delivery after checkout." Most users completed the tasks successfully, but a few struggled with the placement of the filter button and misunderstood the meaning of some delivery icons. We recorded sessions, took notes on navigation patterns, and analyzed verbal feedback to prioritize improvements.

Final Iterations

We implemented key changes from testing: clearer icon labels, simplified filter toggles, a progress bar during checkout, and a persistent order status widget. We also refined the layout for edge cases like failed payments or unavailable items. The final product was more intuitive, visually polished, and user-friendly, meeting the original goal of a frictionless, fast, and engaging food ordering experience.
