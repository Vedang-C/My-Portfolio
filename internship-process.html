UX Design Internship — PostNord

A professional UX design project completed during my internship with the Customer Experience UX team at PostNord.

Project Overview

As part of PostNord’s Customer Experience team, I worked with two other UX interns to improve the user experience and redesign a digital service used by PostNord customers. My work covered user research, wireframing, prototyping, UX writing, responsive UI design, and collaboration with developers. The project gave me the opportunity to work within a large-scale design system while designing for real users and real product constraints.

The Challenge

The existing experience of this specific product had areas where users could face unnecessary challenges while navigating the service. Our goal was to understand these pain points, identify what was already working well, and redesign the experience to make key interactions clearer, more intuitive, and easier to complete.

Working within an established product also meant balancing user needs, business requirements, existing design patterns, and technical feasibility rather than simply redesigning the interface from scratch.

Design Challenge

How might we make the experience clearer and easier to navigate while reducing unnecessary challenges and complications for users?

Research & Insights

What we did?

Me and my team of other two interns conducted user interviews and concept testing to understand how customers experienced the existing service and where they encountered problems and some friction. Alongside customer research, we also gathered feedback from different members of PostNord’s UX team to bring perspectives from design, content, design systems, and product requirements into the process.

Design Exploration

Based on the research findings, I explored different approaches to simplify the experience and make the key journey more intuitive. I started by mapping the user flow and creating low-fidelity wireframes, allowing me to focus on structure, hierarchy, and interactions before moving into visual design.

Working alongside the other UX interns, we explored and compared different solutions, iterated based on feedback, and gradually developed the strongest direction into more detailed wireframes.

Final Design

The final direction focused on creating a clearer visual hierarchy, simpler interactions, and a more consistent experience across the feature. I translated the validated wireframes into high-fidelity UI while working within PostNord’s design system and considering responsive behaviour across desktop and mobile.

A key part of the process was balancing visual decisions with usability, accessibility, content clarity, and technical feasibility. I also worked closely with developers to ensure that the final designs could be implemented effectively and that important interaction states were accounted for.

Key design considerations
Visual hierarchy · Responsive design · Design system · Accessibility · UX writing · Interaction states · Developer feasibility

Testing & Iteration

We tested the prototype with users to understand how well the proposed experience worked in practice and to identify areas that still created confusion or friction. The feedback was used to challenge our assumptions and refine the design before moving further toward implementation.

We also conducted feedback sessions with experienced UX designers and specialists within PostNord, using their perspectives to improve areas such as interaction patterns, UX writing, visual consistency, and accessibility.

Test → Learn → Refine → Test again

Rather than treating the first prototype as the final solution, each round of feedback helped us make more informed design decisions and move closer to an experience that was clearer, more intuitive, and technically feasible.

Outcome & Learning

Working on this project gave me a much deeper understanding of how UX decisions are made in a professional product environment. I learned that good design is rarely about finding the perfect solution immediately. It is about questioning assumptions, listening to users, communicating with developers and stakeholders, and continuously refining the experience.

The project also changed how I think about collaboration. Working within a large UX team and closely with developers showed me how valuable it is to involve different perspectives early, especially when a design needs to work within an established design system and real technical constraints.

Research → Understanding real user needs before designing
Collaboration → Working closely with UX designers and developers
Iteration → Designing, testing, learning and improving rather than stopping at the first solution

My biggest takeaway:
Good UX is not just about making an interface look better. It is about making thoughtful decisions that work for users, the business, and the people who have to build the product.

01

Research

I learned that understanding users goes beyond simply asking what they want. Interviews and testing helped me uncover the reasoning behind their behaviour and challenge assumptions I had made during the design process. I learned to use research as a way to validate decisions rather than design based on assumptions.

02

Collaboration

Working closely with designers, developers and stakeholders taught me that good UX is rarely created in isolation. Different perspectives often revealed things I would not have considered on my own. I also learned the importance of aligning with developers early, understanding technical constraints, and communicating the reasoning behind design decisions.

03

Iteration

I learned that the first design is rarely the best one. Testing, feedback and iteration helped me become more comfortable with questioning my own decisions and making changes based on evidence. Instead of trying to get everything right immediately, I learned to design, test, learn and refine continuously.
